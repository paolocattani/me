# Channels

How the same story is split. Do not write three different essays; write one argument at three depths.

## LinkedIn

Native text. No link in the post. Hook in the first two lines (before "see more"). Blank line between beats. Numbered stages when the story is an arc.

Length: roughly 1_200–1_800 characters for a flagship; shorter for a teaser.

The day after a flagship, Paolo comments with the blog/Substack URL. Teasers for later essays may link: the flagship may not.

Carousel (seven slides, one stage) is a remix after the series, not the first format.

Pile for ideas: Notion database [LinkedIn post ideas](https://app.notion.com/p/6cc419d5a62e477695f7a94ec0e3513c). Status values: `idea` → `bozza` → `pronta`. Move to `pronta` only after Paolo approves the copy.

### When to publish

Audience is Italy / EU, weekday office hours. Post between 08:00 and 09:00 Europe/Rome, on a day Paolo can answer comments for the first hour.

Do not post after 20:00, on Saturday, or on Sunday. Friday is fine only if the next promised post is Monday and the copy does not say *da domani*.

If the intro says *da domani*, both posts must fall on adjacent weekdays: intro Monday, flagship Tuesday (or Tuesday → Wednesday). Never intro Friday.

## Blog (canonical)

Long form. 800–1_500 words. This is what Google and the site keep.

Upload Markdown to R2 `paolocattani-blog` under `posts/<slug>.md`.

```markdown
---
title: "La chat è l'ultimo miglio"
date: 2026-11-06
description: "One or two sentences. The claim, not a teaser."
lang: it
series: documentation-drift
series_index: 1
---
```

`/blog` and `/en/blog` currently share `posts/`. Italian posts will also show on the English index until a locale filter exists. Do not block publishing on that.

## Substack

The blog essay, plus:

- 4–6 lines at the top: why this week, in first person.
- A link to the canonical blog URL at the bottom.

Do not maintain a third redaction. If the newsletter intro changes the argument, the change belongs in the blog too.

Publication: not created yet. Until it exists, write the intro in the draft and stop at "ready to paste".
